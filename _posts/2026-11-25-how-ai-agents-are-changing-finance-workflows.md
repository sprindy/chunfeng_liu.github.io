By Chunfeng (Caleb) Liu

Which overdue invoices exceed $10,000? A finance analyst could answer by opening the ledger, filtering for overdue payments and setting an amount threshold. In a FIN X mock-up shown at the event, the analyst asked the question in plain English. The table narrowed from **148 rows to two**. Both invoices stayed on screen, with their due dates and amounts, ready to check.

![Illustration of an analyst reviewing invoices and travel expenses and an engineer reviewing a code change]({{ '/assets/images/agentic-shift-finance/workflow.webp' | relative_url }})

That example captures the first step towards agentic finance. Software can do some of the work between asking a question and deciding what to do next. Three examples from the session show where that helps.

## Finding the transaction behind the number

A dashboard might flag a jump in travel spending and leave the investigation to the analyst. One session mock-up went further. It showed travel spending **38% above the six-month average**, traced most of the increase to flights booked on 12 November and offered a **See transactions** button. The analyst could open the bookings and judge whether the increase was expected. [Event photo of the FIN X examples](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1H6tFXL0rDekc4Dly9C-vVlHSfBDp2VA-/view)

## Deciding what an agent can do next

Finding a failed supplier payment and retrying it are two different jobs. The first helps explain what happened. The second could move money. In another mock-up, the system grouped three failed supplier payments into one suggested action and gave the user a **Review** button. That small design choice matters. An agent can assemble the cases while the person responsible checks whether each payment should go through.

## Building the software behind the service

Aspire described a similar division of work inside its engineering team. It reported using **15 or more AI agents** across its development workflows. Each code repository carries a brief for its agent. The agent uses that context to draft a fix, and an engineer decides whether to merge it. The finance and engineering examples have something in common. In one, a person can inspect the underlying transactions. In the other, they can inspect the proposed code change. Both give the reviewer something concrete to judge. [Event photos](https://drive.google.com/drive/u/0/folders/1ltPaYMmuWXdWkPSDR7JJUAWgRtaYHXrm)
