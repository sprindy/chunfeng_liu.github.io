## Computing scales up and down

Computing expanded from room-sized machines into data centers, personal computers and everyday devices.

AI is following a similar pattern: scaling up in the cloud while moving closer to the user. Hardware provides capacity; software and models turn it into useful capabilities.

(Note: Hybrid computing—combining on-device, edge and cloud processing—is a growing trend. I’ll leave that for a future post to keep this comparison simple.)

![Computing milestones across centralized systems, personal computers, and mobile and embedded devices.]({{ '/assets/blog/ai-in-perspective/qualcomms-next-chapter-1.jpg' | relative_url }})

That evolution frames the business question behind my Qualcomm SWOT.

## Can Qualcomm turn its mobile moat into an edge-AI platform advantage?

Qualcomm faces pressure on handset revenue and declining Apple-related chip business. Its expansion across PCs and tablets, automotive and assisted/autonomous driving, robotics and Physical AI, and cloud AI infrastructure creates additional growth paths.

My interpretation is that smartphones remain a foundation for this strategy. Qualcomm can build on its mobile market position, partners and ecosystem to bring AI into more devices.

## From smartphones to on-device AI

Phones already demand a difficult combination: computing performance, low power consumption, connectivity, cameras and sensors—all within tight thermal limits. Those capabilities also matter in smart glasses, vehicles and robots.

The recent demonstration of StepEdge-Omni 30B-MoE on a Snapdragon smartphone reference device makes the direction tangible. It shows what coordinated advances in chips, storage, software and models can enable. Commercial success still depends on the experience delivered to users.

![Comparison of data-center, personal, and edge AI, including a Snapdragon demonstration of the StepEdge-Omni model.]({{ '/assets/blog/ai-in-perspective/qualcomms-next-chapter-2.jpg' | relative_url }})

## The trade-off between model capability and device constraints

Smaller or compressed language models can make local AI practical, but they must retain enough capability to complete useful tasks reliably. Memory, response time, battery life and cost all matter.

## Where on-device AI could create business value

That creates several potential sources of business value:

- **More value per device**: useful AI features could support premium products and encourage upgrades.
- **Privacy and control**: local processing can reduce the sensitive data sent to the cloud, supported by proper security and permissions.
- **Broader revenue opportunities**: shared technology can serve multiple device categories.
- **Ecosystem advantage**: reliable software and strong model partnerships can make a platform easier to adopt and harder to replace.

![Qualcomm edge AI SWOT covering its mobile ecosystem, dependence on handset demand, expansion opportunities, and competitive threats.]({{ '/assets/blog/ai-in-perspective/qualcomms-next-chapter-3.jpg' | relative_url }})

Coming from embedded software and sensors, this feels familiar: a capable chip is only the beginning. The complete system determines the user experience.

**My strategic view**: Qualcomm’s challenge is to turn its mobile moat into an edge-AI platform advantage—through capable, efficient on-device LLMs, useful applications, strong partnerships and reliable software.

Where will AI create more value for your business: the cloud, the device, the edge, or a combination of them?
