AI is changing how software teams define tasks, build solutions, and verify results. Reflecting on the AUMOVIO presentation slides, I see six practical changes.

## 1. Engineers can delegate more implementation work

![Software engineering before AI and with AI assistance]({{ '/assets/images/ai-software-practices/4.webp' | relative_url }})

Engineers traditionally drive each step using IDEs, libraries, and automated tools. With AI assistance, they can delegate bounded coding tasks, inspect proposed changes, and guide revisions. The quality of the result still depends on clear requirements and effective review.

## 2. Task definition becomes more important

![A vague driver request compared with a task containing goals, code, constraints, expected behaviour, and acceptance tests]({{ '/assets/images/ai-software-practices/1.webp' | relative_url }})

“Fix the driver” leaves too much open to interpretation. A useful task includes the goal, relevant code, constraints, expected behaviour, and acceptance tests. Engineering experience helps turn a broad request into work that AI can execute and people can evaluate.

## 3. An AI agent needs a working environment

![An AI agent connected to instructions, a repository, build tools, tests, memory, and feedback under human oversight]({{ '/assets/images/ai-software-practices/5.webp' | relative_url }})

The slides describe agents through instructions, tools, memory, and feedback. In software engineering, these connect to repositories, build systems, tests, and project knowledge. Teams must maintain that environment and define what the agent can access and change.

## 4. Development becomes a feedback loop

![Goal and context flow into building and testing, followed by review and release with a feedback loop]({{ '/assets/images/ai-software-practices/2.webp' | relative_url }})

AI can propose changes, run checks, examine failures, and revise its work. People and automated checks provide feedback throughout the process. Release decisions require evidence that the result meets the original need.

## 5. Engineering judgement shapes the outcome

![Engineers defining problems, guiding implementation, and verifying results]({{ '/assets/images/ai-software-practices/3.webp' | relative_url }})

Engineers define problems, guide implementation, and verify results. In embedded systems, that includes hardware behaviour, timing, memory, power consumption, and recovery from faults.

## 6. Software tools are evolving for AI agents

![Comparison of human-oriented tools and agent-ready interfaces, including APIs and MCP, project instructions, structured files, commands, and test results]({{ '/assets/images/ai-software-practices/6.webp' | relative_url }})

As agents take on more software work, tools are evolving to provide discoverable capabilities, explicit instructions, structured documents, and actionable results. APIs, MCP, Markdown, and existing tools work together as these practices develop.
