In my last post, I used tap water and pipelines to explain AI subscriptions vs APIs. This time, let’s look at what AI can actually do.

Imagine you run a business selling furniture.

![Four concepts shaping AI: generative AI creates content, agentic AI works toward a goal, physical AI acts in the real world, and AGI generalizes across domains.]({{ '/assets/blog/ai-jargon-101/four-concepts-shaping-ai.jpg' | relative_url }})

## 💡 Generative AI helps you create.

“Write a product description for this chair.”

It generates the copy, promotional images, or a video script.

## 🧩 Agentic AI helps you get things done.

“Research suppliers for this chair and compare their quotes.”

With the right tools and permissions, it can plan the steps, gather information, and work toward the goal.

## 🤖 Physical AI interacts with the real world.

“Pick up this chair and move it to the packing area.”

A robot needs to perceive its surroundings, handle the object, and adjust its movements. A good text answer alone won’t move the chair!

## 🧠 AGI means Artificial General Intelligence.

The proposed goal is an AI that can learn and apply knowledge broadly across different domains, with human-level cognitive capability. It describes the breadth of intelligence—not whether the AI has a robot body.

One detail matters: these aren’t four neat stages where each replaces the previous one. They overlap. A humanoid robot could combine generative and agentic capabilities without being AGI.

With my background in embedded software and sensors, physical AI is especially interesting to me. It connects AI with the engineering challenges I know: sensing, timing, control, and making things work beyond a screen.
