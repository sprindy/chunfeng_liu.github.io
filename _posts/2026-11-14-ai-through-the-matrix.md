“I know kung fu.”

That scene makes downloading a new ability look effortless. But it also raises a useful question: what’s the difference between learning an ability, using it, and getting help to complete a task?

Here’s how I connect the concepts, with Neo as our guide.

![AI explained through The Matrix. The infographic compares pre-training, post-training, and inference, then illustrates RAG as a briefing, MCP as a connector, skills as playbooks, and an AI agent as the mission doer.]({{ '/assets/blog/ai-jargon-101/ai-through-the-matrix.jpg' | relative_url }})

## 🧠 AI model = The brain

Think of the model as Neo’s “brain,” holding the capabilities developed during training.

## 🥋 Training = Building those capabilities

There are two broad stages:

- Pre-training: General education—learning language, knowledge, and patterns from large datasets.
- Post-training: Specialized practice and coaching—like sparring with Morpheus and improving through examples and feedback.

Both update the model’s learned parameters, or “weights.”

## 💬 Inference = Our daily use of AI

Neo applies his abilities during a mission. We use a trained model to answer questions, write code, or analyze information.

Whether you chat through a subscription app or call a model through an API, you’re using inference. The model’s weights usually stay unchanged.

## 📚 RAG = The mission briefing

Imagine Tank finding relevant building plans and passing them to Neo.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation brings relevant information—from company files, books, or the web—into the model’s context to help it answer. It doesn’t require retraining.

## 🔌 MCP = The standard connector

Imagine a common interface connecting Neo’s support system to databases and equipment.

Model Context Protocol gives AI applications a standard way to connect to external data and tools.

## 📋 Skills = The mission playbooks

A rescue checklist explains how to approach the job. Agent Skills similarly package reusable instructions, scripts, and resources.

The skill guides the work; the agent carries it out.

## 🛠️ AI agent = Neo on a mission

Give the system a goal and suitable tools. It can choose steps, take actions, check results, and adjust:

**Plan → Act → Check → Adapt**

The model helps decide. Tools perform actions. The agent coordinates the process.

One detail about that kung-fu scene: downloading an already learned ability is closer to deploying a trained capability than training it. Loading a skill playbook also doesn’t automatically rewrite the model’s brain.

These concepts fit together: capabilities from training, applied through inference, supported by information, connections, tools, and playbooks.
